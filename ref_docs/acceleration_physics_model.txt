# DopplerSim Physics Model: Accelerated Source (Reference)
# Last Updated: 2026-04-24

This document details the mathematical formulation for time-varying Doppler effects 
in the DopplerSim simulator under constant longitudinal acceleration for both 
single-source and multi-source scenarios.

1. MODELING ASSUMPTIONS
-----------------------
- Isotropic Point Emitter: Sources are modeled as isotropic point emitters (monopoles). 
  Directional directivity (beam patterns) is not explicitly modeled.
- Frequency Domain: The Doppler effect is applied as a time-varying frequency scaling 
  factor in the audio resynthesis pipeline.
- Medium: The medium (air) is assumed to be homogeneous with a constant speed of 
  sound c_sound determined by atmospheric parameters.

2. KINEMATIC MODEL
------------------
The source follows a path s(t) with constant acceleration a.

Initial Velocity (t=0): v0
Constant Acceleration: a

Instantaneous Velocity Magnitude:
    v(t) = v0 + a * t
    (Constraint: v(t) >= 0.001 m/s to prevent backward motion)

Displacement:
    s(t) = Integrate[v(t), dt] from 0 to t
    s(t) = v0 * t + 0.5 * a * t^2

Position Vector:
    P(t) = Pc + u * (s(t) - s_cpa)
    where:
        Pc: Point of closest approach (CPA) to the origin.
        u: Unit direction vector of the path.
        s_cpa: Displacement at the time of closest approach.

3. GEOMETRY & RELATIVE MOTION
-----------------------------
At each timestep t:

Relative Position Vector (Mic to Source):
    R(t) = P(t) - P_mic

Distance:
    r(t) = ||R(t)||

Radial Velocity (Instantaneous Projection):
    v_r(t) = (v_vec(t) . R(t)) / r(t)
    where v_vec(t) = v(t) * u.

    Note: v_r > 0 implies the source is moving AWAY from the microphone.

4. DOPPLER FREQUENCY SHIFT
--------------------------
The instantaneous frequency ratio (f_shifted / f_emitted) is derived from the classical 
Doppler formula for a moving source:

    f_ratio(t) = c_sound / (c_sound + v_r(t))

    where c_sound is the speed of sound (approx. 343 m/s).

5. AMPLITUDE & INTENSITY
------------------------
The acoustic amplitude A(t) is modeled using:

A. Geometric Spreading (Spatial):
    A_spatial(t) = 1 / sqrt(r(t)^2 + eps^2)
    (Standard inverse-distance law).

B. Convective Amplification:
    A_convective(t) = c_sound / (c_sound + v_r(t))
    (Models energy concentration in the direction of motion).

C. Combined Amplitude Formulation:
    A_total(t) = K * A_spatial(t) * A_convective(t)

6. RETARDED-TIME PROPAGATION DELAY
----------------------------------
To model the finite speed of sound, sound emitted at time t_emit arrives at the 
observer at time t_obs:

    t_obs = t_emit + [r(t_emit) - r_cpa] / c_sound

Implementation:
- Interpolation: Linear interpolation is used to map non-uniform arrival times (t_obs) 
  back to a uniform observer grid (t_emit).
- Edge Handling: Boundary padding is used for samples falling outside the mapping 
  range (nearest-available sample values).

7. MULTI-SOURCE SYNCHRONIZATION
-------------------------------
In multi-source scenarios, each vehicle i follows its own profile:

Relative Displacement per Vehicle:
    dt_i = t - t_arr_i
    s_i(t) = v0_i * dt_i + 0.5 * a_i * dt_i^2

Each source is rendered independently and mixed linearly (superposition of pressures).

8. DISCRIMINABILITY METRICS (ALPHA)
-----------------------------------
Two metrics quantify the prominence of the acceleration signature:

A. Velocity Change Ratio (Alpha):
    Alpha = |a * T_transit / v0|
    Measures the total change in velocity relative to initial speed over the window.
    Threshold: Alpha > 0.5 (Heuristic for distinct asymmetry).

B. Geometric-Acceleration Dominance (Alpha_eff):
    Alpha_eff = |a * d / v0^2|
    Determines if acceleration effects outweigh geometric Doppler effects near CPA.
    This helps identify datasets where acceleration is kinematically learnable.

9. SUMMARY FOR CROSS-CHECK
--------------------------
For a source moving at constant acceleration 'a':
1. v(t) is linear.
2. f_ratio(t) results in an asymmetric S-curve.
3. Per-vehicle kinematics (vN_kinematics.npy) store the ground truth [t, v_corr, a, alpha_eff].
